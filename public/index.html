Cast the Party a wardrobe department for murder-mystery hosts

Fit every guest for their part before the party.

Each guest at a murder-mystery party gets a character. Guests often don't know how to look the part, and buying costume pieces online is guesswork, so half the table turns up in street clothes.

Pick a character, add a guest's photo, and YouCam renders the character's hair, colour, makeup, earrings and hat on the guest's own face. You get a costume tag: what changed, which pieces actually showed up on that face, and what to buy.

    How the tag is measured

    What changed

    For every layer on the face card, the page draws the picture that went into YouCam and the one that came back at the same size and marks every pixel where a colour channel moved by 24 or more (of 255). The pink overlay is that mask; the percentage is its share of the picture.

    What showed up

    A piece is punched on the tag only if YouCam reported success and at least 0.05% of the picture visibly changed. A "success" that changed nothing is not counted. The hat portrait is a new composition, so it counts on YouCam's success alone.

    Did the hair reach the shade?

    On the pixels the hair-colour layer changed, we take the median colour before and after and compare each with the character's shade using CIEDE2000. Real hair has highlights and shadow, so 0 is not expected; what matters is how far it moved.

    What the app keeps

    Nothing on disk. Photos and results stay in the server's memory for up to 15 minutes so your browser can fetch them, then they are dropped. YouCam keeps uploaded and generated files for 30 days. The demo spends real YouCam units, so it has a daily cap.
